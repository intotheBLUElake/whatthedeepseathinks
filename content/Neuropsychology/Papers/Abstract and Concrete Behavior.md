## Introduction
The point is the impairment of the so called "abstract behavior" in brain damaged patients.
With *abstract* or *concrete* we are referring to capacity levels of the total personality, or *attitude*. They are functional level of integration and they are mutually exclusive, although one can switch between them.
The plane of the activity ==could be either related to the outerworld or related to inner experiences.==
The concrete attitude is realistic, never mediated by discursive reasoning.
The abstract attitude embraces more than the 'real' stimulus in the scope: ==conscious activity in the sense of reasoning and self awarness==.
The abstract attitude (but not the concrete) is the bases for conscious and volitional modes of behavior.

**Three points**:
1) normal person's behavior is concrete as long as it is embedded in and co-determined by abstract attitude
2) which attitudes operate in the foreground depend upon the given situation
3) in pathology, the abstract attitude is lacking.
	"..deprived of the functional control by that "higher" level and acquires an abnormal predominance of an automatic nature which we may call forced responsiveness as to *form* and concreteness as to *content*."
An important point is that even if the abstract attitude is gone or in no shape to operate ==ideas, thoughts are still there==: what ==change is the way of manipulating and operation with idea and thoughts.==
	"Thus we are dealing with a basic personality change."
## Methods of testing for abstract behavior predominantly qualitative
Tests are performance ones, they exlude a procedure based on languaged in their execution.
1) verbalization and speech can be changed qualitatively to a high degree in a patient with a brain disease: there is an *ambiguity* in the use of words
2) (their) performance tests differ from usual test procedure: the idea is that the patient has two strong tendency, to solve the test or get rid of it; in the former case it is not usefull to know that he has solved it, but it's usefull knowing *how* he has solved it.
	"In pathology, test results can be evaluated only by analyzing the procedure by which the patient has arrived at his results."

In patients, a graded quantitatively difficulty may be a qualitative one.
	"..the usual scoring method based on a scale of difficulty which has been standardized on a statistical basis offers no adequate instrument for determining the nature or the degree of impairment in a patient. Unless one takes into account the entire procedure, the specific reasons for the difficulty the patient encounters, one cannot simply read off from a score which tas\ represents a greater difficulty and which a lesser."

## Tests of the ability to assume the abstract attitude
The tests here presented are supposed to:
- determine whether a patient can or cannot use the abstract attitude
- highlight his particular procedure with respect of the concreteness to which he is confined

1) the instructions should not "tie" the examiner 
2) to every subject with an impairment or believed impairment of abstract atttude have to be given the entire test series

The tests are:
- The Goldstein-Scheerer Cube Test 
- The Gelb-Goldstein Color Sorting Test 
- The Gelb-Goldstein-Weigl-Scheerer (GGWS) Object Sorting Test 
- The Weigl-Goldstein-Scheerer Color Form Sorting Test 
- The Goldstein-Scheerer Stick Test
## The Goldstein-Scheerer Cube Test
To determine whether the subject is able to copy colored designs with blocks.
The blocks have four color, blue red yellow white, two sides with two colors, blue/yellow, withe/red. A design to copy is given.
Two possible approches: concrete and abstract.
 ***Concrete***: he responds unreflectively to the task of "copy". He depends upon his figure-ground impression as suggested by the model.
	  "He does not premeditatively break up the design into four separate parts and then seek the corresponding block-sides, but he tries to reproduce his impression of a coherent figure and ground formation by the above naive " total matching.""
  Interestingly, if the person copies the design by matching procedure then he will start in a certain position (that he has not choose, he is obliged to start there), if the person copies the pattern then it's irrelevant where he will start building.
  They propose 5 different processes behind this test.
### Typical failure-responses to the presented model
- concrete deprence upon size
	  "some patients place one single block on top of the model "covering " so to speak with one identically matched block-side (for example, red) the design. It also occurs frequently that the patient begins with a reproduction by one block and then adds the other 3, each spatially apart from the other. That is, he repeats the model four times because he has been told to use four blocks. Or he places one block after the other in one row" ==(is the first a type of closing-in????==)
- concrete dependence upon the total configuration
- concrete dependence upon one global impression of the design without articulating the parts
- concrete dependence upon "impressiveness" of certain color asspects
...
### Typical failure-responses  to the manipulative block material
... inability to change preferred configuration... lacking of a principle of equivalence between blocks
### Directions
1) Ishihara test for color blindness
2) name each color of the bock

4) design showing

### Evaluation

  *enlargement* of the original model is related to the difficulty in abstracting from discrepancy in size between design area and the four bocks. The *liner division* of the model into four cubes is related to abstract from the figure and break it up.
