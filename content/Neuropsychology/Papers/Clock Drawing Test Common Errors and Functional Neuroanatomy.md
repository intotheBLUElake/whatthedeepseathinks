# CDT Errors
Rouleau [^1] categorized five types of errors in addition to size of the clock: 1) graphic difficulties; 2) stimulus-bound response; 3) conceptual deficit; 4) spatial and/or planning deficit; and 5) perseveration.
## Size
HD: higher incidence of small clocks (< 1.5 inches), due to micrographia seen in BG deficits.
AD: higher incidence of large clocks (> 5 inches), due to impairment of executive (FL) and visuospatial (rPL) abilities.
## Graphical difficulties
"..lines are not precise, resulting either in distortions of the clock face or in numbers that are difficult to read. (..) The overall performance may appear inaccurate and clumsy, but the drawing is still usually recognizable as a clock." -> frontostriatal circuits disruption in coordination.
## Stimulus-bound response
"..tendency of the drawing to be dominated or guided by a single stimulus.."
Two types of errors:
- related to the instructions, "set the time at 10 after 11" -> setted at 10 'til 11
- 1 written near the other ones, like near the 11 or the 10
## Conceptual deficits
"Conceptual deficits are defined as a loss or impairment in accessing knowledge of the attributes, features, and meaning of a clock."
- drawing that does not look like a clock (i.e., misrepresentation of the clock): misrepresentation of the clock suggests the unavailability of a correct graphic representation of a clock.
- drawing with hands that do not communicate a time (i.e., misrepresentation of time): misrepresentation of the time (e.g., hands absent, hands inadequately represented, the time written on the clock) suggests a deficit in the knowledge of the feature that confers most of the meaning of a clock.
## Spatial and/or planning deficit
"..errors in the layout of numbers on the clock-drawing (e.g., neglect of the left hemispace, deficit in planning resulting in gaps in number spacing, deficit in spatial layout of numbers in absence of a specific pattern in spatial disorganization, numbers written outside the clock face, numbers written counter-clockwise)."
Check for anosognosia in this case.
## Perseveretion
Continuation or recurrence of activity without an appropriate stimulus.
Numbers or hands can be persevereted.
